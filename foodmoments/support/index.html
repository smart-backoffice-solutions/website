Food Moments — Support & Privacy

Food Moments estimates the nutritional value of your meals from photos and your own words — with an account that is only your email address, and your data stored in the European Union.

Get help: email us at info@smart-backoffice.nl. We reply within a few working days.

Your data. Your meals live in your account, on our servers in the European Union; your iPhone keeps the recent days. To take everything with you, use Settings → Account → Download all my data. To delete everything, use Settings → Account → Delete account; individual meals are deleted in the app. To withdraw your consent to AI analysis or dictation, use Settings → Privacy. For any privacy question or request (access, correction, deletion), email info@smart-backoffice.nl and we will answer within one month.

A new iPhone? Install Food Moments and sign in with your email address — a six-digit code arrives by email, no password to remember — and your meals, photos and profile come back by themselves.

Downloading all your data. Settings → Account → Download all my data has the server pack everything into one zip — every photo, a set of pages you can browse on any computer, and a table for a spreadsheet — and emails you a download link. The link works for 24 hours; the file is deleted from our server within 48.

Deleting your account. Settings → Account → Delete account erases everything we hold — meals, photos, profile, sessions, any waiting export — immediately and irreversibly. Download your data first if you want to keep it.

Where your data is processed. Your account's data is stored on Microsoft Azure in the European Union (West Europe), and a meal analysis runs on our Azure deployment in the EU Data Zone. Online dictation is the one exception: its speech deployment is a global one, so a dictation recording may be processed outside the EU, including in the United States, under the safeguards described in §5 of the Privacy Policy. Type your note instead of dictating, and nothing about it leaves the European Union.

If we ever stop. Food Moments comes from a small company, so §6 of the Supplemental Terms says plainly what happens if we one day stop developing it: a subscription you paid for runs to its end, we announce it first, and Download all my data gives you an export you can keep browsing without the app.

Changes to the privacy policy

13 September 2026 — Use a photo from an earlier meal again. The photo button in the camera now opens a sheet with two sides: your photo library, as before — the app still receives only the photos you pick — and "Other meals", the photos and scanned products of your own meals, so the breakfast you have every day can be added again without photographing or scanning it. A photo used again is copied into the new meal — only the photo and the size measurement taken with it, never the old meal's note or estimate — and a product is added as if you had just scanned it; both count as eaten now. Touch and hold a photo or product there to mark it as a favorite, as in the Photos app; favorites come first the next time you open the sheet, and which ones you marked is kept in your account with your other settings, so your other iPhone and a reinstall have them too. Nothing new leaves your iPhone: the photos and products were already in your account. See §2 of the Privacy Policy.

13 September 2026 — An unfinished meal waits for you, and a dictation no longer waits on the network. If the app is closed, crashes or is ended by iOS while you are adding a meal, it now opens again exactly where you were — photos, scanned barcodes, what you typed — and the same goes for a correction you had started. When you let go of the mic button the screen is yours again at once, and the words drop into your note when they arrive; with no network, the recording waits on your iPhone and is transcribed by itself once the connection is back. So two things are now kept on your iPhone for a while: an unfinished meal or correction until you press Analyze, Apply or Cancel, and a dictation recording only until its text has arrived. Neither is uploaded to your account, both are left out of iPhone backups, and logging out or deleting your account removes them. Where a recording is processed has not changed. See §§2 and 6 of the Privacy Policy.

6 September 2026 — The app measures the plate while you photograph it. Getting the amount right has always been the weakest part of the estimate: the app usually knows what a dish is and often misjudges how much of it there was. Two things now help. Your iPhone works out, by itself and while you are lining up the shot, how many centimetres across the picture actually covers — from the small movements of your hand, and from the depth sensor on models that have one. And the sizes you mention in passing ("our bread is 35 grams a slice") are kept, so the app stops guessing them again every time. Nothing about this changes what you do: same camera, same button, no scanning, no extra step. Photos you pick from your library or that someone shares with you carry no measurement, and neither does a shot where your iPhone could not make a confident one. See §2 of the Privacy Policy.

2 September 2026 — Your settings follow your account. Until now, deleting the app and installing it again brought your meals back but not the way you had set things up: the appearance, when your day starts, your daily goal and your background photos all started over. They are now kept in your account like everything else, so the app comes back the way you left it — and they travel to a second iPhone too. Only your consents stay on the device, because consent belongs to the iPhone that will do the sending. See §2 of the Privacy Policy.

31 August 2026 — Scan a barcode instead of photographing the packet. When the camera sees a barcode, a small yellow button appears under it. Tap it and the product joins the meal without a photograph: you get its name, its pack size and its own picture, taken from the public Open Food Facts database, and the analysis is told exactly what was in the packet. Scan as many as you like — all the ingredients of a dish, if that is how you cooked it — and they sit in the row with your photos, in the order you captured them. The barcode number goes to Open Food Facts; the product picture is fetched by your iPhone and never reaches us. See §2 of the Privacy Policy.

31 August 2026 — Sharing a meal with the person who ate it with you. When two of you eat the same meal, only one has to photograph it: hold the meal card, choose Share, and pick who receives it. The meal goes straight from your iPhone to theirs — over AirDrop, Messages or whichever app you choose — and does not pass through our servers: we never see it and keep no copy of it. Only Food Moments can open the file, so the person receiving it needs the app. A meal shared with you appears in your timeline and is then stored in your account like a meal you photographed yourself. See §2 of the Privacy Policy.

30 August 2026 — Your account is now backed up. Your meals and photos live in your account and your iPhone keeps only the recent days, which makes the account the only complete copy — and until now it had no backup of its own. It has one now: photos and files can be restored to any moment in the last 30 days, and the rest of your account is copied to a dated file every night, kept for 30 days as well — all of it in the European Union, used for nothing else. Deleting your account still erases everything we hold in service immediately; what sits in those backups rolls off by itself within 30 days. See §6 of the Privacy Policy.

30 August 2026 — Crash diagnostics, and the on-device usage statistics are gone. If the app crashes or freezes, Apple's own on-device detection (MetricKit) sends us a technical report — what kind of failure it was, your iPhone model and iOS version, the app version — so we can find and fix it; it carries none of your photos, notes or anything you typed, is kept 90 days and then deleted automatically, and is tied to your iPhone's installation, not your account. Separately, the Usage card that used to show AI cost statistics in Settings, and the local records behind it, have been removed — what a call costs is now answered from our own server-side ledger, not tracked on your phone. See §§2, 4 and 6 of the Privacy Policy.

29 August 2026 — Apple Health, stated precisely; backup references removed. If you take your daily targets from Apple Health, each day's computed energy target — together with the burned- calories figure behind it — is stored with that day in your account, like the rest of your timeline; the Health readings themselves stay on your iPhone and are never sent to the AI. §2 of the privacy policy now says this explicitly. References to the retired in-app backup were removed from the policy, the terms and this page — Download all my data is the way to take everything with you.

29 August 2026 — Your meals move into an account of your own. Food Moments now has accounts: your email address plus a one-time code — no password, no name. Your meals, photos and profile are stored in that account on our servers in the European Union, so a new iPhone brings everything back and deleting the app no longer deletes your data. Settings → Account adds the two buttons that go with that: Download all my data and Delete account. Fair use is now counted per account instead of per installation. See §§2, 6, 7 and 8 of the Privacy Policy.

28 August 2026 — A gateway of our own. Every analysis and dictation request now passes through a small server we operate on Microsoft Azure in the European Union, instead of going straight to the AI service. It exists so the keys to that service are no longer inside the app, where anyone could extract them. It stores nothing you send — photos, notes and recordings pass through and are gone — and keeps only an anonymous identifier for your iPhone that proves a request comes from the real app. See §§2, 3 and 6 of the Privacy Policy.
