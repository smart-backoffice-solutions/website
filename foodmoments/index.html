How it works

Snap. See. Understand. Choose.

Almost no effort in — a lot of understanding out.

1

Snap

Photograph your food — one shot or several. Then add context: type a note, or just hold the mic and talk. “Two fried eggs and the small roll” is all it takes.

2

See

The meal is analyzed for you: energy, protein, carbs, fat, fibre, sugars and salt, dish by dish, with an honest confidence rating. See what's good about it — and what to keep an eye on.

3

Understand

Through the day, your feed fills up and the day bars fill in. You see how your food day is developing and where you stand — before you choose the next meal, not after.

4

Choose

Been good all day? Your bars will show it. So have the dessert — and feel good about it: you're still on target, and it's your own data saying so, not a guess.

Photos first

It starts with a photo.

The photo is the primary source. The analysis reads the dishes, the portions — even a nutrition label or a barcode when one is in the shot. And it doesn't have to be one photo: a meal can hold up to ten. Cooking tonight? Snap the ingredients as you go, then the finished plate — the analysis puts it all together.

Then add your words — your way

Say it. Or type it.

Often the photos are already enough. When they're not — what went into the smoothie, how much of the pan you actually ate — add context in your own words. If you like typing, type. But do try the mic: dictation here runs on the latest speech models, streams in while you're still talking, and holds up even on a noisy street. Either way, your words always win over the photo — the app listens to you.

Press & hold to talk

“Chicken salad with a slice of sourdough — and I only drank half the orange juice.”

Spoken at capture — portions and details flow straight into the estimate.

Actually the dressing was on the side, I barely used any.

Typed, later that day — corrections work both ways: write it or say it, and the numbers are recalculated.

Nutrition beyond calories

Calories are only the beginning.

Every Food Moment is broken down the way a menu would list it — each dish and drink with its own portion and numbers, summed into totals: energy, protein, carbs, fat, fibre, sugars, salt and an estimated weight. And the app tells you honestly how confident it is, so you know when a quick spoken note would sharpen the estimate.

Your personal feed

One meal is a number. A day of Food Moments is a story.

Your meals, drinks and snacks appear over the course of the day — photos first, like a personal feed viewed through nutrition. At the top, the day card keeps the running score: every bar compares what you've eaten so far with the whole day's targets, so a low bar in the morning isn't a failure — it's headroom.

Food is not just calories. It is energy, protein, carbohydrates, fat, fibre — and timing, habits, pleasure and context.

The goal is not perfection or guilt. It is understanding. When you understand what you eat, you can make better choices for yourself.

Have the dessert. Enjoy the pizza. The point is to know what it means in the context of the rest of your day — and then make the next decision with better information.

What we stand for

Eat with context, not guilt.

Good food gives energy, supports health and makes everyday life feel better. At the same time, nobody eats perfectly all the time — and Food Moments normalizes that. This is an app for people who care about themselves, not one that punishes people for eating.

Private by design

Your food. Your data.

No account, no cloud profile. Your meals, photos and history stay on your iPhone.

🔒

Everything is stored on your phone. Photos and notes are sent out only for the nutritional analysis you ask for — with your consent, and never to build a profile. Voice notes can even be transcribed entirely on-device. The details are in the privacy policy, in plain language.
